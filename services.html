01 / Websites

Custom business websites

Problem

If visitors can’t tell what you offer or how to reach you within seconds, they move on.

Fix

  • What you do and where you work — stated on the first screen.
  • Phone-first layouts — built for mobile, then widened for desktop.
  • A clear path to contact you — email or call, within reach on every screen.

Proof

Checked across screen sizes and confirmed with you before anything goes public — this page included.

See a direction first: before a full build is agreed, you review a one-page concept demo — judge the look before you commit. Open the Cascade concept demo

In practice · Illustration

  • Home services: the first screen says what you fix and where you work — like our Cascade concept demo.
  • Food & café: hours, location, and menu first — ordering laid out like a receipt, no surprises.
  • Appointment-based: the booking step sits on the first screen, beside services and hours.
  • Professional services: calm, fast pages that say who you help and how to reach you. Trust before spectacle.

02 / Digital business cards

Digital business cards

Problem

Paper cards get lost — and once printed, your details can’t change when your business does.

Fix

  • Your name and business details — on one mobile-friendly page.
  • Only the links you choose — the contact and profile links you want people to use.
  • One link to share — send it in a tap, instead of handing over a card.

Proof

There’s a working one right here — open Payton’s card and try it yourself.

Open Payton’s digital card

In practice · Illustration

  • Home services: a link you can text after a quote — your name, trade, service area, and a call button.
  • Food & café: one link for your social profile — hours, address, and how to order or book.
  • Appointment-based: your name, your shop, and your booking link — on a page clients can save and pass on.
  • Professional services: an introduction to send after a meeting — your name, role, firm, and the right contact links.

Each project is scoped individually. Pages, features, revisions, timing, and cost are agreed before work begins. A demo is a concept, not a launched website.

How working with us goes

Clear decisions.
From first conversation onward.

A proposed process built around agreement, review, and a deliberate handoff — not a surprise launch.

  1. 01 / Define

    Start with your business.

    Talk through what you offer, who you serve, and what customers should do next — then agree the scope before starting.

    You bring
    What your business does, where you work, and your current website if you have one.
    We do
    Ask questions, outline the pages and features, and shape a concept demo if it helps you decide.
    You get
    A written proposal with scope, timing, and a fixed cost, before any work begins.

  2. 02 / Build & review

    Make the direction tangible.

    We build a draft around the agreed direction; you review the copy, layout, and contact details.

    You bring
    Your text, photos, and contact details, then your changes at each agreed review.
    We do
    Build the draft around the agreed scope and revise it against your list.
    You get
    A working draft to review on your own phone and computer.

  3. 03 / Confirm

    Check before sharing.

    Pages and links checked across screen sizes; final details confirmed before anything goes public.

    You bring
    A final yes on the details: names, numbers, hours, and links.
    We do
    Test the pages, links, and contact actions at phone and desktop sizes.
    You get
    A site that goes public only when you say so.

Common questions

Asked often.
Answered straight.

The short version of what businesses ask us first.

What does a website cost?

A custom business website starts as low as $500, depending on pages and features (draft figures, pending founder confirmation). Each project is scoped individually, and you’ll know the full price before we start, not after. See starting points.

How long does a project take?

Timing is agreed per project as part of the scope. Three steps — define, build & review, confirm — so the handoff is never a surprise launch.

What do you need from my business?

Three things to start the conversation:

  • What your business does and where you work
  • What you need customers to find or do
  • Your current website, if you have one

What is a demo — is it a real website?

A demo is a concept shaped around your business so you can review a direction before deciding on a full build. It is not a launched website, and nothing goes public until a build is agreed and confirmed with you.
